# Candidate brief format

A brief is read in under a minute: by the coordinator writing an email, or by
an interviewer five minutes before an onsite. Four sentences of substance,
every fact linked.

```markdown
## <Candidate name>

**Now:** <What they're working on currently: latest paper, project or role. 1 sentence, linked.>

**Best known for:** <Most-cited paper, most-used repo, or a notable talk. 1 sentence, linked.>

**Why it connects:** <1 sentence on how their work relates to ours. Specific, not flattering.>

**Suggested opener:** "<One sentence for the email that names their actual work.>"

**Confidence:** <verified | likely, and what each rests on>
```

## Good vs. not

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "An impressive researcher with a strong background in AI" | "Their latest paper filters 40M web clips for motion quality before captioning" |
| "Huge fan of your work!" | "Your motion-quality filter paper is very close to problems we work on every day" |
| A paper found by name only | Leave it out, or ask the coordinator to confirm it's the same person |

## Where briefs get used

- First outreach and scheduling emails (the opener)
- The interviewer packet for an onsite, so the tech deep dive starts at the
  interesting part
- Pairing the candidate with a coffee-chat teammate who works on something
  close

Never in a brief: compensation, age, family, health, religion, politics,
anything from personal social media, or a guess.
