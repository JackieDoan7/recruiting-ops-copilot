# Entryway reset: a worked example of an office fix

How to turn something you notice on a walk-through into a fix with an owner,
a shopping list and a routine. The example: a shoes-off office.

## What's wrong

Everyone takes their shoes off at the door and puts on slippers. There are a
couple of shoe racks, not enough for the team, so shoes pile up on the floor.
The slippers come from bulk packs: one size, thin, shared, and they end up
scattered around the office because no pair belongs to anyone.

It's also the first thing every candidate and guest sees.

## What good looks like

Walk in, see exactly where shoes go, put on a clean pair that fits, and know
where it goes at the end of the day. Nothing on the floor by 6 PM.

## The fix (no extra room needed)

1. **Go vertical.** Wall-mounted shoe racks or slim tilt-out shoe cabinets use
   wall space instead of floor space. Measure the wall first, then size the
   order so there's a spot for every teammate plus room for guests and the
   next few hires.
2. **Give every teammate their own pair.** Washable slippers in their own
   size, kept in a labeled spot. Ownership is what keeps them off the floor:
   a shared one-size pair belongs to no one. Collect sizes with a one-question
   form, and ask new hires in their first-day checklist.
3. **Sized guest pairs.** A small bin per size (S, M, L, XL) of washable guest
   pairs, with a hamper right next to it for used pairs.
4. **Better slippers, fewer of them.** Look for machine washable, closed toe,
   a non-slip rubber sole, and real sizes instead of one size. They cost more
   per pair than a bulk pack and last much longer, and they're what candidates
   and guests actually wear. **Try before buying for everyone:** order 3
   kinds, let a few teammates wear them for two weeks, and buy the one people
   actually like.
5. **Make it routine.**
   - End of day: a two-minute sweep, strays back to their spots.
   - Weekly: wash the guest hamper; count guest pairs during the supply count.
   - Twice a year: replace worn teammate pairs.

## How it shows up in the tools

- **Supply Autopilot, Inventory tab:**
  - The slipper trial is a fixed target of 6 pairs (3 kinds, 2 each). Once
    the team picks a winner, add it as a *Scales with: Team, one each* row,
    so a bigger headcount automatically orders a pair for each new hire. The
    name labels for each teammate's spot already work that way.
  - Guest pairs use *Guests, one each*, so a week with 12 onsite candidates
    has 12 clean pairs plus a cushion.
  - Racks, bins and the hamper are one-time purchases: a fixed target with
    what's already on hand, so they order once and never again.
- **Supply Autopilot, Office fixes tab:** tracks this project with an owner
  and a status, next to anything else teammates flag.
- **Onsite Scheduler:** the itinerary email tells candidates it's a shoes-off
  office and asks for their shoe size, so a fresh pair is waiting for them.

## How to know it worked

- Before and after photos of the entryway, taken at the same time of day.
Check back two weeks after the change:

- No loose pairs by the door at 6 PM.
- The slipper trial picked a pair people actually like.
- Ask three teammates whether it's easier.
